## 6. Solar Gravity

To find the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of the Sun and determine how much your weight would increase, we can follow these steps:

### 1. Acceleration Due to Gravity on the Sun ($g_S$)

The formula for the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of a spherical body is given by Newton's law of universal gravitation:


$$g = \frac{G \cdot M}{R^2}$$

Where:

* $G$ is the gravitational constant $\approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$
* $M_S$ is the mass of the Sun $= 2 \times 10^{30} \text{ kg}$
* $R_S$ is the radius of the Sun $= 6.96 \times 10^8 \text{ m}$

Substitute these values into the formula:


$$g_S = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}^3\text{kg}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2}) \times (2 \times 10^{30} \text{ kg})}{(6.96 \times 10^8 \text{ m})^2}$$

First, calculate the numerator:


$$G \cdot M_S = 1.3348 \times 10^{20} \text{ m}^3\text{s}^{-2}$$

Next, calculate the denominator (the square of the radius):


$$R_S^2 = (6.96 \times 10^8)^2 = 4.84416 \times 10^{17} \text{ m}^2$$

Now, divide the numerator by the denominator:


$$g_S = \frac{1.3348 \times 10^{20}}{4.84416 \times 10^{17}} \approx 275.56 \text{ m/s}^2$$

So, the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of the Sun is approximately **$275.6 \text{ m/s}^2$**.

---

### 2. Weight Increase Factor

Your weight is directly proportional to the acceleration due to gravity ($W = m \cdot g$). To find out by what factor your weight would increase, compare the Sun's gravity to Earth's average surface gravity ($g_E \approx 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$):

$$\text{Factor} = \frac{g_S}{g_E}$$

$$\text{Factor} = \frac{275.56 \text{ m/s}^2}{9.81 \text{ m/s}^2} \approx 28.1$$

### Conclusion

* The acceleration due to gravity on the Sun's surface is **$275.6 \text{ m/s}^2$**.
* Your weight would increase by a factor of **$28.1$ times** (meaning you would weigh more than 28 times what you do on Earth).
